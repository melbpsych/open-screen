1. Prepare

Open Assessment. Its compact Menu holds navigation and Save/Resume. Choose Clinical or Forensic and select relevant measures. Saved presets expand when available. Enter a reference before answering if history is needed. Measure information buttons show purpose, burden and evidence limits.

Choose Initial staged screening for brief questions followed by indicated full forms, or Repeat monitoring for planned full measures. Confirm any measure-specific permissions and form choices. All measures are listed on the Measures step. Forensic starts with response patterns selected; you can deselect them. Save the current selection as a favourite on that page, or manage presets under Menu → Options. Favourites remember practice selections only, never patient notes or permissions.

Enter the patient reference before answering if history comparisons are needed: the reference locks after the first response, including when blank. Review the response-task range and expand Before you start before handing over the device.

Open Menu → Options to choose screening or monitoring and automatic or manual advancement. Return to Measures, then select Continue to Ready. Review the selected measures and answering mode, select Start assessment and hand the device to the person. This is a shared-device workflow; the clinician controls are not protected by a login.

2. Answer questions

Read the question and timeframe. Automatic mode advances on selection; manual mode uses Continue. Use Back to edit. Free text also uses Continue. Page actions stay at the bottom while you scroll.

When a question cannot be answered, choose Prefer not to answer or Unable to answer. These responses remain missing and affected scores stay incomplete. Nothing is imputed as zero. After brief screening, review the initial findings and choose which longer questionnaires to complete. Shared questions are not repeated; each option shows how many remain. The number of questions can change as follow-ups open.

Each item keeps its source timeframe visible, including after recovery. Choose Pause or ask for help to stop and contact the supervising clinician without making a missing-answer choice.

At the end, return the device to the psychologist. Scores are presented in the clinician review.

3. Optional risk notes

Screening continues without automatic safety interruptions. Risk assessment and recording are at the clinician’s discretion.

To add a note, open Notes & final record → Risk notes (optional). One free-text note is enough. Additional risk details remain available when useful, including for existing structured records. No risk field, follow-up plan or acknowledgement is required to copy, export or finalise a screening record.

Recorded questionnaire responses remain accurate, including an earlier PHQ-9 item 9 endorsement after a correction. Empty risk documentation does not produce warnings or empty report sections.

4. Review & finalise

Open Review clinician record. Scores lead the page. Recorded item-level observations and incomplete results remain visible. Open the summary preview to inspect the note, or select Copy summary to paste them into your clinical notes. Select a measure row for its full graph, scoring details and evidence. Missing results stay labelled incomplete. A below-gate result does not exclude a condition. Unadministered forms are not measured zeros; personality and response-pattern descriptions do not establish diagnosis or credibility.

Clinical notes, context and follow-up details are optional. Add only what is useful for your screening record.

Finalisation is optional. To create a locally attested record, expand Notes & final record, identify the reviewer and confirm review of the screening record. Selected questions must be completed; explicitly missing answers remain unscored. No safety documentation or context checklist is required. Later edits create a draft and preserve the preceding final in history. Local attestation does not authenticate identity.

Follow-up actions, owners and dates can be recorded independently. Open Screen does not schedule reminders.

5. Save & resume

Before closing

After finalisation, choose Save assessment. For drafts, open Save & export → Resume later → Save encrypted assessment (or Save progress during questions). Then choose a passphrase of at least 12 characters and check the downloaded .openscreen file in approved storage. Keep its passphrase separately. There is no password recovery or automatic patient-data backup.

The save-status line compares the tab with its last downloaded or restored encrypted snapshot. It cannot confirm that your device stored the file successfully. Later changes need another save. Keep the tab open until you have checked the file.

Select the logo to return home. With unsaved progress, choose Save progress, Leave without saving or Keep working. Save progress downloads an encrypted file, then returns home automatically. Keep the file and its passphrase to resume later, and check that the browser stored the download. Escape, cancelling the save dialog or a save error preserves your current work. Other Save controls keep the assessment open.

If the bundle reaches its encrypted size limit, choose Archive encrypted history. Allow multiple downloads and verify every numbered .openscreen part. Each part unlocks with the chosen passphrase and can be resumed independently. Nothing is removed automatically. After verification, Remove archived history from this tab requires a separate confirmation and keeps the current assessment. Save that current assessment again.

To resume, choose Resume assessment on the homepage or Menu → Resume in setup, select the encrypted file, enter its passphrase and confirm the displayed patient reference. Check that it is the correct person before replacing current work.

Copy summary is available above the results. It copies a concise note with scores, relevant review findings and any entered notes. It remains Draft until finalised and does not save an encrypted file. Export PDF beside Copy summary opens the browser’s print dialog. Choose Save as PDF (or your device’s PDF/share option) and all pages. The complete PDF includes selected measures and results, recorded observations and optional notes, available history, sources, and the full record and response appendix. Screen disclosures do not need to be expanded. In Save & export, Clinical report contains the offline HTML report; Structured data contains JSON and the full record. These exports are unencrypted clinical records. Store and transfer them according to practice policy. The response appendix is optional for HTML and JSON; PDF always includes it. Exporting or cancelling a PDF does not mark an encrypted assessment as saved. Draft exports remain labelled Draft; finalisation does not itself save a recoverable file.

Version 0.5.0 rejects 0.4.0 and earlier assessment files. Open older files with their original release and export there; do not edit version fields. No cross-version migration is supplied.

6. Repeat monitoring

From a final record, select Start repeat monitoring. This retains the patient reference, goals and previous finals, creates a fresh assessment and clears answers and review. Confirm permissions again. Selected full forms stay available at low brief scores, subject to published stop rules.

Save the new encrypted assessment to retain both visits. Expand Assessment history, then a measure to see its chart and values table. History compares matching forms and scoring definitions and discloses changed settings. Review differences clinically; graphs do not provide reliable-change or treatment-response estimates.

For PCL comparisons, expand Assessment history and record a non-identifying event reference and reuse it only after confirming the same event. Different or unknown anchors remain separate. Do not repeat a trauma narrative merely to establish continuity.

For a different person use New assessment in Save & export, then check the fresh reference and selections. Never use repeat monitoring for another patient.

7. Practice settings

Open the screening logic page to see the pathways. Select a gate to focus its rule; All thresholds reveals the complete draft without discarding edits. Or open Edit thresholds directly. Enter changes and rationale, review, then activate the revision. Draft previews do not change active assessments. Reset controls restore field or practice defaults.

Settings are local to this browser. New assessments use active preferences; existing assessments retain their captured rules. Export the change record for practice governance. Published locked rules cannot be edited. A custom threshold is a practice choice, not a new validated cutoff.

Choosing measures & clinical use

Choose measures for the referral. Symptom scores support enquiry; they do not diagnose conditions, establish credibility or predict offending.

Safety and conditions needing direct assessment

Assess suicide/self-harm, threats, access to means, intoxication/withdrawal and safeguarding independently of questionnaire gates. A negative screen is not safety clearance. Record needs, findings, the plan and who will follow up; avoid a global low/medium/high suicide prediction. NICE self-harm guidance ↗

Possible mania, psychosis, severe withdrawal or eating-related medical compromise need direct assessment and the appropriate clinical pathway. GAD scores do not cover all anxiety disorders. An ASRS result alone cannot diagnose ADHD.

External forms and risk findings

External totals do not import item responses or trigger another form. Review any PHQ-9 or EPDS self-harm endorsement directly, even with a low total. The external C-SSRS yes/no note records endorsement, not official triage. If you choose to document it, use optional risk notes or the additional structured details. C-SSRS guidance ↗

Forensic interpretation and population fit

Record the psycholegal referral, collateral, language/interpreter and administration adjustments. Separate the person’s account, observations and independently supported findings. Clinical cutoffs do not establish capacity, responsibility, violence risk or malingering. IPIP response patterns are not performance or symptom validity tests. Use appropriate specialist methods for those questions.

Additional measures and permissions

C-SSRS healthcare/EHR embedding and K6/K10 use have author permission with applicable attribution. They are candidates for a separate exact-form implementation, not newly administered here. WHODAS electronic use requires licensing. Existing external results remain available. K6/K10 author terms ↗ · WHODAS terms ↗

8. Access & troubleshooting

Keyboard and reading preferences

Tab moves between controls. Within a response group, arrows, Home/End or numbers select an option. In automatic mode that selection advances immediately; choose manual mode if you need to explore options first. Enter/Space activates buttons; Escape closes dialogs and Save & export. On a phone, use Close at the top of the export panel. Use the skip link to bypass navigation.

Browser zoom and text enlargement are supported. Layouts reflow on narrow screens and in landscape. Reduced-motion and system high-contrast preferences are respected; scores have text equivalents. There is no time limit.

The file will not unlock

Check the file, passphrase and application version. Wrong passphrases, damaged or incompatible files are rejected. Retain the original file; do not alter its contents. Cancellation keeps the current assessment.

The app is unavailable or the page was closed

Keep an active assessment open if an error occurs. Invalid practice preferences block new assessments but leave Resume available for a compatible captured file. Review/reset those preferences separately. For other startup problems, ask your local administrator to check configuration and server availability. After a tab is closed, only a previously saved encrypted assessment can restore its responses. HTML/JSON exports are records, not resume files.

Devices and accessibility support

Use a current browser supporting JavaScript modules and JSON imports. Automated checks cover Chromium, Firefox and WebKit with phone emulation; physical devices, screen readers and practice workflows still require human acceptance. Record the browser, device, task and steps when reporting a problem, using synthetic examples rather than patient information.
